# Service categories & niches — observed across researched AI consultancies

Side deliverable for Kentro's service-menu decisions. Source: 27 validated small US AI/agent consulting firms (of 70 checked, of 877 extracted from Coresignal). Raw per-firm data: `research/validation.json`. Frequencies below = number of the 27 firms offering it (as labeled by validators; clustered here into normalized categories).

## A. Service categories (normalized, by prevalence)

### Core menu — what nearly everyone sells
1. **AI strategy & roadmap** (~18 firms incl. variants: audit, discovery, blueprint, opportunity assessment) — the universal entry service. Undifferentiated on its own; the strong firms wrap it in a named framework or fixed-scope productized offer.
2. **Custom agent / LLM application development** (~15 incl. agentic systems, multi-agent orchestration, voice agents, chatbots) — the most common build offer in 2026; "agentic" is now the default vocabulary.
3. **Workflow & process automation** (~6 incl. RPA, document workflows, revenue-ops automation) — often positioned by business outcome (hours saved) rather than technology.
4. **RAG / knowledge systems** (~4: vector search, chat-with-your-docs, semantic search) — frequently a named sub-offer under agents.
5. **Intelligent document processing** (~3: classification, extraction, validation, ERP/AP/TMS integration) — less crowded than agents; the best practitioner (Hexaa) pairs it with an ROI calculator and vertical focus (logistics, AP, insurance).

### Production & operations tier — where "engineering-led" firms differentiate
6. **MLOps / deployment / monitoring** (~5 incl. edge, private, on-prem/air-gapped) — evals + observability language separates real engineering shops from slide shops.
7. **Managed AI operations / "operate" retainers** (~4) — running agents after launch; recurring revenue and a natural continuation of fixed-scope builds.
8. **AI governance, security & compliance** (~5: SOC2/RBAC/audit trails, HIPAA, NYDFS, AI CoE setup, policy) — heavy in regulated-vertical firms.
9. **Data engineering & classic ML** (~6: pipelines, predictive modeling, CV/NLP) — usually a substantiating capability, not the headline.

### People tier — services sold around the humans
10. **AI enablement & training** (~7: team training, executive coaching, workshops, playbooks, change management).
11. **Fractional AI leadership** (~8 (!): fractional CAIO, embedded AI lead, ongoing advisory retainer) — notably common among solo/2-10 firms; monetizes seniority without headcount.
12. **Staff augmentation / AI engineering pods** (~3) — adjacent to consulting; several firms treat it as a separate revenue line.

### Emerging / differentiated offers (each seen once, worth watching)
- **SEO/AEO/GEO** — optimizing clients to be recommended *by* AI assistants (Anveshi's "Share of Model") — a genuinely new category.
- **Unit-economics optimization for AI systems** (cost/task, model routing, caching) — HelloAgentic; resonates with technical buyers.
- **Readiness diagnostics as products** (free scored scans, gap scorecards) — FirmAdapt, InitializeAI; doubles as lead generation.
- **Named accelerators / reusable IP** (SQL-to-PySpark converters etc., GenAI Protos) — productized artifacts framed as buyable.
- **AI-native product studio work**, 3D digital twins, post-quantum-crypto-ready sovereign AI — edges of the space.

## B. Engagement models observed (the conversion ladder)
Free self-serve diagnostic (scored scan / calculator / assessment) → **fixed-price, fixed-duration entry engagement** (e.g. $1,500 roadmap; fixed-fee 3-4-week Blueprint; 30-day audit with week-by-week deliverables) → fixed-scope build → operate retainer / fractional leadership. Risk-reversal add-ons seen: performance guarantees, liability coverage, response-time SLAs, "you own the code/IP", cloud-neutrality pledges. The pattern is consistent: **the firms that convert make the first step cheap, concrete, and named.**

## C. Niches (verticals & buyer types, by frequency among 27)

**Verticals:** healthcare (10) · financial services/finance/banking (9 combined) · insurance (3+2 P&C/banking overlaps) · manufacturing (4) · retail/e-commerce/CPG (6) · government & public sector (3, incl. SCIF/sovereign) · education (3) · legal (2, incl. family law) · construction (2) · media & entertainment (2) · logistics/AP/back-office (2) · professional services (2) · energy/utilities (2) · plus one-offs: agritech, commercial real estate, crypto/digital assets, pharma.

**Buyer types:** SMB owners · mid-market executives · PE-backed companies (pre-exit ops cleanup, portfolio value creation) · enterprise software/engineering teams needing AI muscle · regulated-industry compliance owners · boards/leadership needing AI literacy.

## D. Implications for Kentro (observations, not decisions)

- The crowded center is "AI strategy + custom agents for everyone." Differentiation in the strong set comes from **(a) a productized, named entry offer, (b) a proof device that shows work, (c) a vertical or buyer-type wedge** — rarely from the service list itself.
- **IDP is under-supplied relative to agents** and matches Kentro's stated capability; Hexaa demonstrates the winning presentation (vertical + calculator + integration story).
- **Construction is nearly empty** among these firms (2 mentions) — and Kentro already ships Agent K into construction. That's a defensible niche signal worth considering.
- **Fractional AI leadership and operate-retainers** are how small firms build recurring revenue on top of project work — candidates for Kentro's menu later, without contradicting the "we build production systems" positioning.
- **Healthcare/finserv** are the most contested verticals; entering there requires the compliance vocabulary tier (evals, audit trails, SOC2-readiness honesty).
