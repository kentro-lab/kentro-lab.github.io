§ 02 Notes from production

Things we learned the hard way.

Short field notes from our team's prior work. Details are generalized and clients are not named; we would rather share a pattern honestly than dress up a number.

ExtractionN-01

The last five percent is where the project lives.

Our team has shipped extraction pipelines where the model handled the clean documents easily. The work — and the value — was in the edge cases: rotated scans, missing fields, and the vendors whose formats never quite match. A confidence threshold with a review queue beat chasing a higher headline accuracy.

GroundingN-02

A wrong answer with a citation is worse than a refusal.

On grounded assistants, our team found that users trusted an answer as far as its source. Retrieval quality, honest "I don't know" behavior, and a visible link back to the passage mattered more to adoption than any single model choice. We now design the grounding and its failure modes before the conversation.

OperationsN-03

The launch is the start of the real work.

Our team has learned that agents drift as inputs, prompts, and tools change underneath them. Without evaluation you can rerun and traces you can read, a regression is invisible until a user finds it. The systems that stayed reliable were the ones we could observe, measure, and roll back on a bad day.

These describe our team's prior experience, generalized and anonymized. Kentro AI Labs is a new studio; we do not publish client names, testimonials, or metrics we cannot stand behind.

§ 03 Method

What we insist on before anything ships.

A prototype is a question. Production is the answer, and it has a checklist. These are the practices we hold to on every build, not extras we quote separately.

Evaluations

Evaluate before launch, and keep evaluating.

We build a representative test set from your real cases and gate releases against it. A change that improves one thing and quietly breaks another should fail the gate, not the customer.

Observability

Every run is traceable.

Inputs, tool calls, model outputs, and costs are logged so you can answer "why did it do that?" after the fact. Operating a system you cannot see into is guessing.

Rollback

A safe path back.

Prompts, models, and tools are versioned, and changes ship behind a switch we can reverse. When something regresses — and eventually something will — recovery is a decision, not an incident.

Ownership

You own what we build.

The code, the prompts, and the data are yours, documented for a team that is not us. We would rather build something you can run than something that needs us to keep breathing.
